# Source corroboration

[Adoption](../adoption.md) · [Source integration](../guides/knowledge-source-integration.md)

## Purpose and applicability

Apply when a consequential factual conclusion depends on sources that may be deceptive, obsolete, or mutually dependent. Traceable citations alone cannot establish trustworthy or independent support.

## Requirement

Before reliance, define material claims and the required strength of support. Inspect source identity, edition, competence for the claim, relevant context, and common origins. Seek contradictory evidence within a declared search scope. Count repeated or derived reports as one underlying evidence stream where applicable. Record unresolved conflicts and insufficient support; withhold or qualify the affected conclusion rather than inventing corroboration.

## Implementation

1. Assign a competent reviewer and set evidence needs proportionate to the decision. A firsthand record may be sufficient for a narrow claim; no universal source count applies.
2. Record source revision, relevant passage, relationship to the subject, derivation, and limits. Retrieve primary evidence where needed and available.
3. Compare editions and conflicting accounts. Document search scope and access gaps; inspect whether apparent agreement comes from a shared original.
4. Record the inference from observations to conclusion and its uncertainty. Reassess when a source correction or new contradiction changes support.

## Expected outcome and assessment

Expected outcome: claimed corroboration reflects distinct, suitable evidence and unresolved contrary evidence stays visible.

Review a supported narrow claim, two copies of one report, an obsolete edition, an authentic source that contradicts its citation, and independent conflicting reports. Include unavailable primary material.

- **Pass:** supported claims remain usable within scope; copies are not counted as independent support; outdated, contrary, and unavailable evidence changes the disposition as required by the predeclared evidence needs.
- **Fail:** known shared origins, material conflicts, or inadequate support are hidden while the conclusion is accepted as established.
- **Inconclusive:** necessary source identity, context, or review records cannot be inspected.
- **Evidence:** claim/source map, derivation links, search scope, conflicting passages, reviewer reasoning, target revision, time, and dispositions.

## Dependencies and limitations

Requires source access and subject competence. Complements [evidence traceability](evidence-traceability.md), which tests whether a claim has inspectable support, and [retrieval coverage](retrieval-coverage-qualification.md), which bounds search completeness. Corroboration cannot prove truth or rule out coordinated deception.
